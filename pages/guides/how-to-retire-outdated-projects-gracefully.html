Here’s the honest take on “How to retire outdated projects gracefully” — written for people who want a link that works harder than a PDF.

People delay publishing until it feels perfect. Meanwhile, peers with simpler sites are already circulating a URL.

This guide is for builders polishing the details that change first impressions who want a site that feels intentional — not another generic template dump.

The real problem

A website helps you stand out because it compresses your story into something shareable, memorable, and updatable — qualities a static attachment can’t match.

Do this next

  1. Screenshot your homepage and mark the first thing the eye hits
  2. Move proof higher; move biography lower if needed
  3. Publish the change even if it’s imperfect

Share like a professional

Put the link in applications, mentor emails, and community intros. A portfolio that nobody visits is still a draft.

How a website helps you stand out

Small craft decisions — photo quality, copy length, load speed — compound into “this person seems sharp.”

Field checklist

  • Delete anything you can’t defend in an interview
  • Test on a phone before you share
  • Ask one mentor for a 60-second reaction

Try this today

Open your draft (or start one), apply one change from the list above, and publish. Momentum compounds faster than waiting for “ready.”
