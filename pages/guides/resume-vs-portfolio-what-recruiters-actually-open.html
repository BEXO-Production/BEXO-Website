Resume vs portfolio: what recruiters actually open

Leah Fontaine · 7 min read · 2025-03-03

Unsplash — resume and laptop

Resumes get you past filters. Portfolios win the human on the other side of the screen.

Students over-optimize keywords and under-invest in the moment a real person decides “interesting” vs “next.”

This guide is for candidates designing for how recruiters actually read who want a site that feels intentional — not another generic template dump.

The real problem

If they open anything after your resume, make sure it rewards curiosity in under ten seconds.

Do this next

  1. Treat the resume as a map; treat the portfolio as the destination
  2. Mirror job titles and skills across both so nothing feels inconsistent
  3. Use the Hire Me page for structured facts; use project pages for proof

PDF + site, not PDF or site

Keep the PDF for portals that demand uploads. Use the live site for humans. Put the URL in the resume header so both assets point to each other.

Design for the skim

Assume ten seconds. If education, skills, and contact aren’t obvious, a busy recruiter won’t hunt. Pair a designed home with a Hire Me page that prints cleanly.
